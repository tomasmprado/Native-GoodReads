Features

Everything you do on Goodreads, nicer.

Your shelves, ratings and friends stay on Goodreads, so nothing changes there. The app just makes them quicker and more pleasant to use.

Home

What you're reading, your goal, your reading streak and a gentle weekly goal, the next volume of what you've started — and a race with your friends to your reading goals.

All your shelves

A list, a wall of covers or a bookcase of spines in each book's own colours, with sorting and filters by rating, year and genre. A work's volumes show as one book. Drag books between shelves; undo if you slip.

Rich book pages

Click any book for its synopsis, rating, genres, publisher, awards, series, reviews and the friends who read it.

Suggestions that know you

A taste profile from your stars scores books from readers of your favourites, friends who loved what you loved, your series and authors. Each one says why — or swipe through them in Discover, a feed that learns from every swipe.

Friends

See what friends are reading, browse their shelves and compare tastes — where you agree, and what they loved that you haven't read.

Stats & goal

Your yearly goal (synced with your Reading Challenge), books and pages per year, favourite authors and genres.

Your Year in Books

Your year told like a story, one big fact at a time — then a card with it all, ready to save and share.

A Bingo for the year

A 4×4 card built from what you rarely read — new countries, old books, genres you skip. Squares fill themselves as you finish books.

Letters to yourself

Write a few lines when you finish a book; they come back in a year. And what you were reading on this day, years ago.

Notes & highlights

Private notes and quotes for every book. Bring in your highlights from Apple Books, Kobo, Calibre or a Kindle in one go.

Gentle reminders

A daily nudge to read — type how far you've got right into the notification. Optional alerts for friends and new books.

Fits into your Mac

Scan a book's barcode with the camera, your library and notes in Spotlight, Add to Goodreads from Safari's Share menu, works offline (changes go to Goodreads when you're back), and a CSV export.

Apple Books, Kobo & Calibre

What you read in your reading apps comes over to Goodreads: progress, finished books, highlights — and books you have that aren't on a shelf yet.

Your own copies

Add an EPUB or PDF to any book — or let Calibre's be yours — then open it in Apple Books where you left off, or send it to your Kobo when you plug it in.

Profiles & authors

Click any reader or author for their page — shelves, challenge, favourite genres, series, quotes, and a link to the author's Wikipedia article.

Book details

Everything about a book, one click away.

Click a book anywhere in the app — a shelf, a search, a friend's update — and its page opens.

  • Synopsis and rating — with how readers' stars are spread.
  • The facts — publisher, dates, pages, format, ISBN, translator and illustrator.
  • Series — where it fits, and the next book, one click from Want to Read.
  • Friends who read it — and the stars they gave.
  • Rate and review it right there, and keep your own notes.

Install

Up and running in a minute.

The app isn't on the App Store, so a small installer sets it up on your Mac — no warnings, no repeated password prompts.

Download

From the latest release, download Goodreads.dmg and install.sh into your Downloads folder.

Run the installer

Open Terminal (Applications → Utilities), paste this and press Return:

bash ~/Downloads/install.sh

Sign in

The app opens. Click Log In and sign in on Goodreads' own page — your password goes only to Goodreads. If your Mac asks for your login password once, choose Always Allow.

Use Homebrew?

Install it from the tomasmprado/tap tap. The last part signs the app on your Mac, so the Keychain stops asking for your password after updates:

brew tap tomasmprado/tap && brew trust tomasmprado/tap && brew install goodreads && bash "$(brew --repo tomasmprado/tap)/install.sh" --resign
Rather not use Terminal?

Open the .dmg and drag Goodreads to Applications. The first time, right-click the app and choose Open (or go to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway). Your Mac may ask for your Keychain password again after updates.

Privacy

Your reading stays yours.

  • You sign in on Goodreads' own page. The app never sees or stores your password.
  • Your notes and progress history live only on your Mac — with a one-click backup.
  • No tracking, no ads and no account of its own. It talks only to Goodreads, Google Books, Wikidata (where authors are from), Apple Books and Wook (for prices) and GitHub (for updates).
  • Open source — every line is on GitHub.

What's new in 3.2

A feed that learns, streaks, and offline.

  • Discover learns from every swipe — and from the books you open or linger on — re-ranking as you go, with something different now and then.
  • Reading streaks and a gentle weekly goal on Home, with a new widget that nudges you on days you haven't read.
  • Works offline: your changes wait and go to Goodreads when you're back online.
  • The next volume of what you've started, a bookcase in your books' own colours, and Homebrew.

Questions

Good to know

Does it cost anything?

No. It's free and open source.

Do I need a Goodreads account?

Yes — the app shows and changes your own Goodreads library. Everything you do in it shows up on Goodreads too.

Which Macs does it run on?

Any Mac with macOS 13 Ventura or later, Apple silicon or Intel. On macOS 26 it uses the new Liquid Glass look.

How do I update it?

When a new version is out, an Update button appears at the bottom of the window. One click shows what's new and installs it.

Search finds little, or says its quota is used up.

Add a free Google Books key in the app's Settings — the shared one is nearly always busy. Get one at console.cloud.google.com: enable the Books API, then Credentials → Create Credentials → API key.

Something looks wrong or empty.

Goodreads sometimes changes its website. Open Settings → Troubleshooting → Log, copy it, and open an issue.

Every feature is explained in the guide. Another question? Ask on GitHub.
