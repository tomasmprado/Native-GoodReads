Install

You need a Mac with macOS 13 Ventura or later — Apple silicon or Intel.

  1. From the latest release, download Goodreads.dmg and install.sh into your Downloads folder.
  2. Open Terminal (Applications → Utilities), paste this and press Return:
bash ~/Downloads/install.sh

The installer copies the app to Applications, tells macOS it's safe to open, and gives it a signature that stays the same between versions. The app opens when it's done.

The first time, your Mac may ask for your login password so the app can use the Keychain. Type it and choose Always Allow — it won't ask again.

Without Terminal

Open the .dmg and drag Goodreads to Applications. The first time, right-click the app and choose Open (or System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway). The Keychain prompt may come back after each update.

With Homebrew

The app is in the tomasmprado/tap tap. Homebrew asks you to trust a tap before installing from it:

brew tap tomasmprado/tap && brew trust tomasmprado/tap && brew install --cask native-goodreads

The app isn't notarised by Apple, so the cask takes off the "downloaded from the internet" flag, as the installer does. To get the signature that stays the same between versions — so the Keychain stops asking after updates — run once:

bash "$(brew --repository tomasmprado/tap)/install.sh" --resign

The app updates itself; brew upgrade --greedy works too. brew uninstall --zap --cask native-goodreads removes it with its data and settings.

Other installer options

CommandWhat it does
bash install.sh path/to/Goodreads.dmgInstalls from a .dmg (or .app) somewhere else.
bash install.sh --resignRe-signs the app already in Applications — useful after updating from 2.5.0.
bash install.sh --remove-certificateRemoves the signing certificate the installer made.

Removing the app

Drag Goodreads from Applications to the Bin, then run bash ~/Downloads/install.sh --remove-certificate if you used the installer.

Sign in

  1. Click Log In at the bottom of the sidebar.
  2. Sign in on Goodreads' own page, exactly as on the website. Your password goes only to Goodreads; the app never sees it.
  3. Your shelves appear in the sidebar.

The foot of the sidebar shows Signed in with a green dot. Sign Out is in Settings → Library, and clears everything the app saved for that account — except your notes and progress history.

Tip: add a free Google Books key in Settings. Search works without one, but the shared free key is almost always busy, and with your own you get more details about each book.

The window

Sidebar

Home, Suggestions (with Discover and Bingo), Friends, Stats and Notes (with Letters), then every shelf you have with how many books it holds.

Toolbar

The magnifying glass on the left opens Search (⌘ K). Panes with parts have a switch in the middle; on the right, actions for the pane on screen — layout, sort, filter, export and reload on shelves.

Foot of the sidebar

Quiet unless there's something to say: an action that failed, Undo when you can, being offline, an update, and Log In when you're signed out.

Settings

In tabs — General, Notifications, Reading Apps, Library and Troubleshooting. See Settings.

Closing the window doesn't quit the app: it keeps running (click its Dock icon to bring the window back) so notifications, syncing and widgets stay up to date. On macOS 26 the app uses the Liquid Glass look.

Home

The first thing you see when the app opens. It always fits the window — nothing to scroll:

  • Currently Reading — a card for each book you're reading, with its cover, a progress bar and how far you've got. Click a cover for its details; right-click for Update Progress, Mark Read & Rate, Stop Reading and View Details.
  • Your goal — books read this year against your Reading Challenge goal, counted the way Goodreads counts them.
  • The race — you and your friends running towards your own reading-challenge goals, with a finish line and a marker for where an even pace would put you today.
  • Your week — pages read, days in a row and books finished this month.
  • Next — what to read next: the top of your Want to Read order, or a pick from authors you've read lately.
Finish by a date: in a book's Update Progress, set a date you want to finish by and Home (and the reading reminder) tell you how many pages a day that takes.

Shelves

Click a shelf in the sidebar. Saved shelves appear instantly and refresh in the background; the reload button in the toolbar fetches it again now.

List, covers or bookcase

The first toolbar control switches between a list (with title, author, your stars and dates), a wall of covers and a bookcase (every shelf but Currently Reading): spines with the title and author, thicker for longer books, on wooden planks. Click a spine to pull it out and see a little about the book underneath; + opens its details. Double-click opens them straight away, and right-click has the usual actions.

Volumes together

The volumes of one work — "Os Irmãos Karamázov I" and "II", or "Guerra e Paz – Volume I" — show as one book with the first one's cover and, on the right, how many you've read of those on your shelves (1/2). Each volume's actions are in its own submenu. In your own order on Want to Read they stay apart, so you can drag them one by one.

Tidy titles and names

Titles and authors are tidied wherever they show: the stray * Goodreads puts in some names, double spaces and odd symbols go; names and titles typed all in capitals get proper capitals, with words like "de" kept small; "Retorno, O" becomes "O Retorno".

Sorting

The sort button orders the shelf by As Shelved (Goodreads' order), Title, Author, Rating, Date Read or Date Added.

Your own order for Want to Read

On Want to Read there's also My Order: drag books into the order you'll read them. The first one shows on Home as Up Next. Clear any filters first — you can only drag when every book is showing.

Filtering

Type in the toolbar's search field to narrow the shelf by title or author. The filter button narrows it by your rating, the year you read it, or genre, and holds the smart lists. Genres appear for books whose details the app has seen — open a few, or use Stats → Fill In.

Moving books

Drag a book onto another shelf in the sidebar to move it there. Want to Read, Currently Reading and Read are exclusive — a book is on one of them at a time. Your own shelves stack: adding a book to one keeps it where it was.

Smart lists

Ready-made views of any shelf, most useful on Want to Read. Open the filter button and choose under Show:

ListShows
Short booksBooks under 250 pages, shortest first.
Highest rated on GoodreadsBooks with a Goodreads average of 4 stars or more, best first.
Waiting the longestEverything, oldest addition first.
By authors you loveBooks by authors you've read three or more books by, or rated two of four stars or more.

Short books and highest rated need page counts and ratings. If some are missing, the same menu offers Get page counts and ratings, which looks them up for the whole shelf (from Goodreads, or Google Books when Goodreads won't say).

Book actions

Click a book anywhere — a shelf, search, suggestions, a friend's shelf or update — to open its details. Its ••• button (or a right-click) has everything else:

ActionWhat it does
Update Progress…On Currently Reading: set a percentage or a page. See Reading progress.
Stop Reading…On Currently Reading: see Stopping a book.
Mark Read & Rate…Stars, the date you finished and an optional review, all at once. The best way to finish a book.
RateJust the stars, for a book already on a shelf.
Move toAny of your shelves.
View Details…The book's page in the app.
Open on GoodreadsThe book's page on the website.
Share…Its Goodreads link, through Messages, Mail and so on.
Remove from ShelfTakes it off this shelf.
Undo: after moving or removing a book, an Undo button appears at the foot of the sidebar for a few seconds.

Reading progress

Choose Update Progress… on a Currently Reading book — on Home, a shelf or a reminder notification.

  • Percent — drag the slider. When the page count is known, it shows which page that is.
  • Page — type the page you're on; the app shows the percentage.
  • Note — optional, posted with your update on Goodreads.

The app remembers every update (Goodreads only keeps the latest). That history is what the finish date and pages a day on Home come from, and it stays on your Mac even if you clear the cache.

Stopping a book

Not enjoying it? Choose Stop Reading… from the book's menu or its card on Home.

  1. Write why, if you want to remember.
  2. Choose where it goes on Goodreads: back to Want to Read, or off your shelves.
  3. If you have a shelf called dnf, did-not-finish, abandoned or similar, it's added there too.

The app keeps how far you got. Stopped books are listed at the bottom of Stats with Resume Reading (back to Currently Reading) and Forget. They never count as read.

Book details

Everything about a book on one page:

  • Rating — Goodreads' average, how many ratings and reviews, and how the stars are spread.
  • Synopsis — with Show More for long ones.
  • Genres, and details: publisher, publication date, first published, pages, format, language, ISBN, original title, and who edited, illustrated or translated it.
  • Awards, the main characters, and where it's set.
  • Series — the series and the book's number in it, with the next book and a one-click Want to Read.
  • Where to Get It — one click searches for the book at Amazon (for your country), AbeBooks (second-hand) and Libby/OverDrive (libraries). In Portugal, Wook and Bertrand too.
  • Friends Who Read This — with the stars they gave, or "Reading now". Click one for their profile.
  • Your rating and review — post them right there. Like everywhere in the app, rating a book marks it as read.
  • My Notes — see Notes.
  • Reviews — a few from other readers.

Click the author's name for their bio, stats and other books. Share at the top sends the book's link.

Details come from the book's Goodreads page. When Goodreads doesn't have something — or won't send it — the app fills the gaps from Google Books.

Pick for Me & Rate Unrated

  • Pick for Me — in Want to Read's toolbar. Shows one book at a time from what's on screen (so filters and smart lists narrow it down). Another (or Space) shows a different one; Start Reading moves it to Currently Reading.
  • Rate Unrated — in Read's toolbar. Your books without stars, one at a time: press 1–5 to rate, S to skip.

Search

Click the magnifying glass at the top of the window, or press ⌘ K. Type a title, author or ISBN. Results appear as you pause typing; Return searches straight away.

  • On Your Shelves comes first: books you already have, with the shelves they're on.
  • Then other books — add one to a shelf or mark it read from its ••• menu.
  • Filters under the search field narrow by language, sort by newest, or restrict to a subject (like "history" or "poetry").

Scan a barcode

File → Scan a Barcode… (⇧ ⌘ B), or the barcode button in Search. Hold the book's barcode up to your Mac's camera — or an iPhone's, through Continuity Camera, which is used when there is one. The barcode is outlined once it's read; then put the book straight onto a shelf, or see which shelf it's on already.

Suggestions

Suggestions in the sidebar has three parts, switched at the top: Suggestions, Discover and Bingo.

  • Picked for You — a taste profile from your Read shelf (authors and genres weighed by your stars against your own average, and by how recently you read them, minus books you gave up on) scores books from several places at once: Goodreads' "Readers also enjoyed" for your favourites, friends' five-star books, the next book in your series, more by authors you love, and well-rated books in your genres. Books several favourites point to rise to the top, each says why, and there are two per author at most. Books in languages you don't read, and books you already have under another title, are left out.
  • Your Series — how far you are in each series you've read from, with the next book (one click to Want to Read). A notification tells you when a new book joins one of them.
  • New from Your Authors — books out in the last year or coming in the next, by authors you've read more than once. Checked once a day.

Settings controls which language suggested books should be in. The reload button rebuilds the list.

Discover

Suggestions one card at a time, with the cover, rating, why it was picked and a description. Swipe or use the keys:

SwipeKeyDoes
Right→Want to Read
Left←Pass
Up↑I've read it — then rate it
—SpaceThe book's details (double-click works too)
—⌘ ZBring back the last book

Every swipe is remembered and teaches the suggestions what you like: books you said yes to lead to more like them. When the deck runs low, more are found in the background.

Bingo

A 4×4 card for the year, built from what you rarely read: countries and continents you've never visited, books older than your grandparents, genres you skip or keep passing in Discover, books waiting on your shelves for years, authors new to you. Squares fill themselves as you finish books this year. Click one for ideas from your Want to Read, or to tick it yourself; a full row, column or diagonal is a bingo. New Card deals another.

Friends

What your Goodreads friends are reading and their latest updates. Click a book for its details, or a friend to open their profile:

  • Their stats and recent activity.
  • Collections — browse their Want to Read, Currently Reading and Read shelves, with their stars.
  • Compare Tastes — how many books you've both read, how often your stars agree, where you disagree most, and the books they loved that you haven't read yet.

The app also reads your friends' Read and Currently Reading shelves now and then, which is what powers Friends Who Read This in book details and the avatars on Home. Friends with private shelves won't appear.

Profiles & authors

Click a reviewer's or friend's name or picture — in reviews, comments, friends' updates — for their profile: their details and recent activity, reading challenge, favourite genres and books, what they're reading, every shelf, quotes, friends and their Year in Books.

Click an author's name anywhere for their page: where they were born (and died), influences, the full bio, their works and series (with the books in each), quotes, reviews and followers, plus a link to their Wikipedia article — in Portuguese when there is one.

Where to get it

A book's details have a Where to Get It menu: Apple Books (with its price, when it has the ebook), Kindle, Kobo and the usual stores.

Add your own in Settings → Reading Apps → Stores: a name and a search address with {bookname}, {author} or {isbn} where the book goes — for example https://www.wook.pt/pesquisa?keyword={bookname}.

Turn on price alerts in Settings → Notifications to hear when a book on Want to Read gets cheaper — the ebook on Apple Books, and at Wook in Portugal.

Stats & goal

  • Reading goal for this year: how many books you've read, whether you're ahead or behind, and a chart by month. If you've joined the Goodreads Reading Challenge, its goal is used automatically; otherwise set it right there.
  • Books read, average rating, books you're reading, and pages this year.
  • Books per year, how you rate, and your most-read authors.
  • Pages and genres: pages per year and your top genres. Fill In looks up the books the app doesn't know yet.
  • Stopped Reading: books you set aside, with how far you got.
  • Authors' Countries: a world map of where the authors you've read come from, looked up on Wikidata by itself.
  • Reading Time: time spent reading this year and a calendar of the days you read, from your Kobo's reading time, the days a book was open in Apple Books, and progress updates.

Your Year in Books

The gift button in Stats. Your year plays like a story, one big fact at a time: books and pages, your busiest month, top genre and author, the shortest and longest book, your average rating against everyone's, the most popular book and your hidden gem, the highest rated, the first and latest, and your five-star books.

Click, or press → / Space, for the next one (← goes back). It ends on a card with the whole year — Save Image… keeps it as a picture to share. Pick another year at the top.

Notes

Every book has a private My Notes box in its details: write your thoughts, and add favourite passages as quotes. Notes save as you type and stay only on your Mac (they're included in backups and CSV exports).

The Notes pane

Notes in the sidebar has two parts, switched at the top: Notes and Letters. Notes gathers your notes, quotes and highlights, grouped by book, newest first. They also show up in Spotlight.

  • Search by words in the quote, the title or the author.
  • Click a book's name for its details.
  • Each quote's ••• menu: Copy (with the title and author), or Share as Image… for a card you can save or send.

Letters & On this day

When you finish a book, the Mark Read sheet has room for a few lines to yourself. They're sealed and come back a year later, with a notification. In Notes → Letters, Write a Letter lets you write about any book at any time and choose when it opens: in a month, six months, a year or five years. Letters stay on your Mac; the number of unread ones shows next to Notes in the sidebar.

The same pane shows On this day: the books you finished, or were reading, around today's date in past years.

Importing highlights

Choose File → Import Highlights…, then where they come from:

  • Apple Books — what you highlighted in the Books app on this Mac. macOS will ask to let the app read them; if it doesn't, choose Choose Apple Books Folder… and click Open.
  • Kobo — plug the Kobo in by USB; its highlights and notes are read straight from it.
  • Calibre — what you highlighted in Calibre's e-book viewer.
  • Kindle — connect the Kindle by USB and pick documents/My Clippings.txt.

The app matches each book to your shelves by title and author and lists what it found. Untick anything you don't want, then Import. Highlights become quotes and notes are added to the book's notes. Importing again never creates duplicates.

Your copy of a book

Every book's details have a Your Copy box. Click Add EPUB or PDF… (or drop a file on it) to keep your own copy with the book.

  • Open in Apple Books opens it where you left off. The first time, Apple Books takes the file in; after that the app opens the same book again, so you never start back at page one.
  • Books with a file go to your Kobo when you sync it.
  • Books from Calibre get their file by themselves, without being copied.

The ••• menu has Show in Finder, Replace… and Remove. Removing a Calibre file only unlinks it — the file stays in Calibre.

Apple Books, Kobo & Calibre

File → Reading Apps… (or Settings → Reading Apps) looks at what you've read in Apple Books on this Mac, a Kobo plugged in by USB and your Calibre library, and lists what Goodreads is missing:

  • Further along — books on Currently Reading where you've read more in the app.
  • Started — books on Want to Read that you've begun.
  • Finished — books you finished that aren't on Read.
  • Not on your shelves — books you have that Goodreads doesn't know you have.

Click each one's button, or Do All. Books are matched to your shelves by Goodreads id, ISBN, then title and author. Progress only ever goes forward, and anything you change back on Goodreads stays changed.

In Settings → Reading Apps you can let it run by itself (a minute after the app opens, then every six hours): Sync progress and finished books, and Add books that aren't on my shelves.

macOS may ask before the app can read Apple Books. If it doesn't, choose Choose Folder…, press ⌘ ⇧ G, paste ~/Library/Containers/com.apple.iBooksX/Data/Documents and click Open.

Syncing a Kobo

Plug your Kobo in by USB and a notification offers to Sync with Goodreads (or choose Settings → Reading Apps → Sync Kobo…). You see everything first, and nothing changes until you press Sync:

  • Send to Kobo — books on Currently Reading or Want to Read that have a file, into a Goodreads folder on the Kobo.
  • Remove from Kobo — books the app sent that you've since finished. Only books the app sent are ever removed.
  • Bring over to Goodreads — progress, finished books, highlights and notes.

Eject Kobo lets you unplug it safely. Turn the notification off in Settings → Reading Apps.

Calibre

If you use Calibre, the app finds the library Calibre last opened (or choose another in Settings → Reading Apps → Calibre) and:

  • matches its books to your shelves — by the Goodreads id Calibre's metadata download adds, the ISBN, or title and author;
  • uses each book's EPUB (or PDF) as your copy, left where it is in Calibre;
  • brings over where you stopped in Calibre's viewer, and its highlights (in Import Highlights);
  • lists books that aren't on a shelf, to add to Want to Read with a click — never by itself.

Write ratings and shelves back to Calibre (off unless you turn it on) puts your stars, a tag like Goodreads: Read and the Goodreads id into Calibre, keeping its other tags and identifiers. Calibre can't be changed while it's open, so this waits until it's closed — or click Update Calibre Now after quitting it.

Widgets

Right-click the desktop → Edit Widgets → search for Goodreads. They also work in Notification Center.

Currently Reading

Small, medium or large: your books with covers, progress, pages and finish dates.

Reading Goal

A ring with books read this year against your goal, and whether you're ahead.

Quote of the Day

One of the quotes you saved, a different one each day.

Friends' Race

You and your friends in this year's Reading Challenge.

Widgets show what the app last saw, and refresh whenever something changes in the app.

Shortcuts & Siri

In the Shortcuts app, search for Goodreads:

ActionWhat it does

Update Reading ProgressSets a book's percentage.

You can also ask Siri, in English — for example "What am I reading in Goodreads" or "Update my reading progress in Goodreads".

Safari & Share

On a book's page in Safari — a shop, a review, a library — choose Share → Add to Goodreads. The app finds the book by the ISBN on the page (or its title) and adds it to Want to Read.

Notifications

All optional, in Settings. They're checked while the app is running, even with its window closed.

  • Friends — when a friend starts or finishes a book. Checked every hour.
  • New books — when an author you read has a new book, or a new book joins a series you follow. Checked once a day.
  • Kobo connected — offers to sync when you plug a Kobo in. On unless you turn it off.
  • Price drops — when a book on Want to Read gets cheaper on Apple Books, or at Wook in Portugal.
  • Letters — when a letter to yourself opens.
  • Reading reminder — a daily nudge at the time you choose, about the book you're reading. Type a percentage straight into the notification to update your progress.

Spotlight

The books on your shelves, and your notes and quotes, show up when you search with ⌘ Space. Choosing one opens it in the app. Turn it off in Settings to remove them.

Export & backup

Export to a spreadsheet

Settings → Library → Export Library… saves every shelf to one CSV file, close to Goodreads' own export format, including your notes. The export button in a shelf's toolbar saves just that shelf.

Backup

Your notes, progress history and stopped books exist only on your Mac — Goodreads has nowhere to keep them. Back them up:

  • By hand — Settings → Backup → Back Up… (or File → Back Up Notes & Progress…) saves a .goodreadsbackup file. Restore… puts one back, replacing what's on the Mac.
  • Automatically — turn on Back up automatically every week. Backups go to iCloud Drive › Goodreads Backups (or a folder you choose with Change…), and the last eight are kept. Back Up Now makes one straight away.

Settings

Open with ⌘ , or Goodreads → Settings. It's in tabs:

TabWhat's there
GeneralBook language for suggestions, how far "recently read" reaches, your Google Books API key (a free key for better search and details — get one at console.cloud.google.com: enable the Books API, then Credentials → Create Credentials → API key; kept in your Keychain) and the reading goal (your Goodreads Reading Challenge, or your own).
NotificationsFriends starting and finishing books, new books by your authors, Apple Books price drops, the Kobo notification and the daily reading reminder.
Reading AppsSyncing Apple Books, Kobo and Calibre, and your own stores.
LibraryYour account (Log In, Sign Out, Export Library), backup, Spotlight, and the cache — how much space the app uses to stay fast, and Clear Cache (safe: your notes and progress are kept).
TroubleshootingThe Log, and a Browser button (off unless you turn it on) that shows the Goodreads page the app uses behind the scenes.

The app's language follows your Mac's: English or European Portuguese. To change it for this app only, go to System Settings → General → Language & Region → Applications.

Text size

Make text bigger or smaller anywhere in the app with View → Bigger Text (⌘ +) and Smaller Text (⌘ −). Actual Text Size (⌘ 0) goes back to normal. The app also follows your Mac's text size (System Settings → Accessibility → Display → Text size) where macOS passes it on to apps.

Updates

The app checks for new versions on its own. When one is out, an Update button appears at the foot of the sidebar. Click it to read what's new, then install: the app downloads the update, replaces itself and reopens, keeping its signature so the Keychain doesn't ask again.

Privacy

  • You sign in on Goodreads' own page; the app never sees or stores your password.
  • Shelves, ratings and reviews live on Goodreads. The app keeps a copy on your Mac so it opens instantly.
  • Notes, letters, progress history, stopped books and your swipes in Discover are only on your Mac (notes, progress and stopped books are in backups you make).
  • Your Google Books key is kept in the Keychain.
  • The app only talks to Goodreads, Google Books, Wikidata (where authors are from), Apple Books and Wook (for prices) and GitHub (for updates). No tracking, no ads, no account of its own.

Troubleshooting

The Mac keeps asking for my Keychain password

Run bash ~/Downloads/install.sh --resign and choose Always Allow once.

"Goodreads" can't be opened

Use the installer, or right-click the app → Open, or System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.

Search finds little, or says the quota is used up

Add a Google Books key in Settings.

A shelf or friends look empty or out of date

Click the reload button in the toolbar. If it's still wrong, Goodreads may have changed its website: open Settings → Troubleshooting → Log, copy it, and open an issue with it. Turning on the Browser button there shows the Goodreads page the app is using behind the scenes.

A book has no synopsis or details

Close it and open it again — the app retries Goodreads and fills the gaps from Google Books. A Google Books key helps here too.

The widget is empty

Open the app once; widgets show what it last saw.

I'm offline

The app shows what it saved last time and says so at the foot of the sidebar. It refreshes by itself when you're back online.
